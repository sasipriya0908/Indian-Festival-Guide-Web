EID AL-FITR 🌙

A celebration of gratitude, community, and peace

Introduction

Eid al-Fitr, often called the "Festival of Breaking the Fast," is a significant religious holiday celebrated by Muslims worldwide. It marks the end of Ramadan, the Islamic holy month of fasting (sawm).

Significance

Eid is a time of gratitude to Allah for the strength and self-control provided during Ramadan. It emphasizes unity, forgiveness, and the importance of charity (Zakat al-Fitr) to ensure everyone can join the celebration.

The Celebration

The day begins with special communal prayers. People wear new clothes, visit friends and family, and exchange "Eidi" (gifts or money). It is a day of joy, where streets are filled with festive energy and the spirit of brotherhood.

Traditional Cuisines

Known as the "Sweet Eid," popular dishes include Sheer Khurma (vermicelli pudding), Biryani, Kebabs, and various traditional sweets shared with neighbors and the needy.
