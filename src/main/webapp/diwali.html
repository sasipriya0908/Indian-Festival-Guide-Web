DIWALI 🪔

Celebrating the victory of light over darkness

Introduction

Diwali, also known as Deepavali, is the Festival of Lights celebrated across India and by the Indian diaspora worldwide. It is one of the most significant and popular festivals, symbolizing the "victory of light over darkness, good over evil, and knowledge over ignorance."

History & Significance

In Northern India, Diwali marks the return of Lord Rama to Ayodhya after 14 years of exile and his victory over the demon king Ravana. To celebrate his return, the people of Ayodhya lit thousands of oil lamps (diyas), a tradition that continues today.

The Celebration

During Diwali, people decorate their homes with lamps, candles, and colorful rangoli patterns. Families gather for Lakshmi Puja, exchange sweets and gifts, and celebrate with spectacular fireworks displays. It's a time for new beginnings and spreading joy.

Traditional Cuisines

No Diwali is complete without a variety of sweets (Mithai) including Laddu, Gulab Jamun, Kaju Katli, and savory snacks like Murukku and Chivda.
