RAKSHA BANDHAN 🎀

Celebrating the eternal bond of love and protection between siblings

Introduction

Raksha Bandhan, commonly known as Rakhi, is a traditional Hindu festival celebrated annually across India and by Indian communities worldwide. The name translates to "the bond of protection," signifying the sacred promise, love, and lifelong support shared between brothers and sisters.

Significance

The festival honors the unique sibling bond. It has deep roots in historical accounts and Hindu mythology, most notably the story of Draupadi tying a strip of her saree on Lord Krishna's injured finger, after which he vowed to protect her from all harm. Today, the festival transcends biological relationships to symbolize universal sibling care and mutual support.

The Celebration

During the key ritual, sisters perform a ceremonial aarti, apply a tilak on their brothers' foreheads, and tie a decorated thread called "Rakhi" around their wrists. In return, brothers offer gifts and traditional sweets, and solemnly pledge to stand by their sisters. Families dress in vibrant traditional attire, gather for festive meals, and exchange blessings.

Traditional Cuisines

Sweets play a central role, with popular treats including Kaju Katli, Ghevar, Gulab Jamun, and Coconut Ladoos. A grand family lunch is often prepared, featuring savory dishes like Puri-Chana, samosas, paneer delicacies, and rich saffron Kheer.
